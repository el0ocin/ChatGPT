# Pricing Checklist

Use this after the AI draft and before posting.

## Quick sale indicators
- broad local demand
- seasonal timing helps now
- bulky item you want gone
- mid-value household item
- price-sensitive buyer pool
- many local comps

## Max profit indicators
- niche buyer pool
- strong brand or rarity
- high original retail
- limited local supply
- collector or enthusiast appeal
- patient timeline is acceptable

## Price review steps
1. Compare the AI list price to the floor price the client gave you.
2. Make sure the list price creates room to negotiate without breaking the floor.
3. Check whether the item needs a fast local move or a patient sale.
4. Check if the item category is seasonal in the current market.
5. Confirm the headline value proposition justifies the price.

## Default pricing rules
- Use clean, believable local pricing.
- Prefer prices ending in 5 or 9 when it feels natural.
- Do not use a "max profit" price if the client clearly needs fast cash.
- Do not set the list price so close to the floor that every negotiation becomes painful.

## 48-hour pricing review
If an item is still live after 48 hours:
- low views: strengthen title and lead line first
- decent views, weak replies: adjust price slightly
- lots of interest: hold firm
- default reduction: 5% to 8%

## Never do this
- invent unrealistic retail anchors
- price off national shipped listings without adjusting for local pickup
- make a huge drop on day one
- bury the item's best differentiator under filler text

## Final check
Before posting, ask:

`Would a local buyer looking at three similar listings understand why this one is priced this way in the first two lines?`
